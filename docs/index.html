Who is Nick

Born into chess in 2008, when Chess.com was still small enough that Daniel Rensch hadn't yet registered. Nick beat him to it. The account is older than Danny's. He keeps the screenshot.

Magnus Carlsen, in a moment of plausible distraction, accepted a friend request. That, too, has been preserved.

On the board: an obstinate 1.d4 player with white, a Caro‑Kann specialist with black, and a long-standing project to dismantle the egos of overrated opponents. Peak ratings hover around 2300 bullet, 2150 blitz, with an appetite for closed positions and a deep prejudice against gambits.

“Loves crushing stronger players' ego.” — self-described

This bot is what happens when you take 22,887 of his most considered moves, run them through a neural network, bolt a tactical safety net to its policy head, and turn it loose on the internet.

How it works (briefly)

1

Opening book

A 100,000-position book built from Nick's actual games. Frequency-gated, so only positions he reached in 10+ games are answered from book. Goes 30 plies deep into mainlines.

2

Maia2 policy

A 23M-parameter neural network fine-tuned on his moves. Top-1 move-prediction accuracy: 47% (up from 25% on the unmodified model). The personalisation signal is real.

3

Stockfish referee

Maia proposes its top five candidate moves — already in Nick's distribution — and Stockfish at depth 10 picks the tactically soundest of those five. Style preserved, blunders curtailed.
